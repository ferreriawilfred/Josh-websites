Jesus Said “Let This Cup Pass From Me.”
He Drank It Anyway.

This Is Carved From the Wood of That Garden.

Everything else I’ve offered you today moves outward.

Your prayer, inscribed on holy parchment, traveling to the Stone.

The water, the rosary, the crucifix — all of them going somewhere, carrying something forward.

This one is different.

This doesn’t go anywhere.

It comes home to you — from the most decisive moment in the history of the Christian faith.

The night before the cross, Jesus stood in the Garden of Gethsemane and called it a cup.

“Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me; yet not my will, but yours be done.”Luke 22:42

The cup of suffering.

The cup of the cross.

The cup of everything that was coming.

He asked for it to be taken away.

The sweat fell like drops of blood.

An angel came to strengthen Him.

And then He drank it.

That same night — before He walked into the garden — He had already told His disciples what the cup meant:

“Drink from it, all of you. This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins.”Matthew 26:27-28

Two cups. One night.

The cup of His suffering, accepted in a garden.

The cup of the covenant, offered at a table.

The cup is the central object of the entire Christian faith — it is what He called His pain, and what He used to offer His love.

And this chalice is carved from the olive trees of the garden where He held His.

Handcarved From the Olive Wood of Gethsemane.

The same ancient trees.

The ones rooted in that garden since the first century.

The ones that witnessed the agony, the sweat, the angel, and the surrender.

A wooden chalice, shaped by hand from wood that grew from the soil He knelt on.

When you fill this chalice and drink from it, you are drinking from a cup carved from the garden where He drank from His.

When it sits on your table or your nightstand, it is an object from the place where He said yes to the cross for you — present in your home every day, not just in a photo on your phone.

“I will lift up the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord.”Psalm 116:13

That is what this cup is for.

A man from Atlanta wrote to me after his arrived.

He was in the middle of the worst season of his life — a diagnosis, a marriage that was coming apart, a business failing after fifteen years.

He said he filled the chalice with water on the first morning he had it and drank from it slowly.

He thought about Gethsemane.

About what it meant to hold a cup and choose to drink it anyway.

He said: “I stopped fighting the season I was in. I did what He did in that garden. I said yes to the cup.”

Three months later he wrote again.

The marriage was rebuilding.

The last scan came back better than his doctor expected.

He still drinks from the chalice every morning.

He said it’s the most honest prayer he knows how to say.
